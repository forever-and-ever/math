What is matrix mortality?#

For fixed positive integers d and k, Md(k) asks: given at most k integer d × d matrices, does some nonempty product equal the zero matrix?

Ai1 Ai2 ⋯ Ain = 0, n≥1.

The entries and the length of the product are unbounded, and factors may repeat in any order, so a product is a word over the k generators. Mortality is semidecidable: enumerating products by length finds a zero product whenever one exists. It is decidable when some algorithm also recognizes every family without one. An undecidability theorem says that no single algorithm is correct on all inputs, not that any particular input is hard.

Two related problems test one coefficient instead of the whole product. Scalar zero reachability Zd(k) supplies a row L and a column C and asks whether LYC = 0 for some nonempty product Y; the structured form Z̊ requires every generator to have first column e1. The corner problem Rd(k) fixes the coefficient to one entry. A reduction from one problem to another transfers undecidability toward the target and decidability back toward the source.

How the proofs work#

Every undecidability proof in this series is a computable map from programs to matrix families that are mortal exactly when the program halts. The map is assembled from five stages, each a translation between problems.

  1. Halting as a word equation. A tag system repeatedly reads the first letter of a queue, appends a word, and deletes a fixed number of letters. Everything deleted, followed by the current queue, always equals the initial queue followed by everything appended. Four word pairs turn this identity into a correspondence problem: a solution is a halting run (M3(5)).
  2. A word equation as one coefficient. Word-pair matrices multiply like pairs of words, and one entry of a product is the difference of the two words’ numerical codes, so it vanishes exactly when the words agree. The four pairs can share coordinates: the paired scalar series uses three matrices in dimension four, and a binary code uses two in dimension six.
  3. One coefficient as a zero product. For a row r and column c, the rank-one separator S = cr satisfies SXS = (rXc)S, so a vanishing coefficient becomes a zero product. More generally, a low-rank generator forces every product through a small space, and interface compression reduces mortality to products there.
  4. Fewer generators, lower dimension. Generator packing spells many generators with few letters and pays in dimension. A transition A and a cut UO present a whole sequence of matrices OAnU, and its least dimension is a Hankel rank; M8(2) chooses the separator to lower that rank.
  5. Integer matrices and computability. Multiplying each generator by a nonzero integer clears denominators without creating or destroying zero products. The whole map must be computable; here it is primitive recursive, and Lean checks it.

Each stage carries three obligations. Every halting run must produce a zero. No other product may vanish, including products of malformed words, of words that begin or end in the middle of an encoding, and of words that use a separator in the wrong place. The translation must be computable and end in integer matrices. Most of every proof is spent on the second obligation.

Decidability table#

Undecidability is monotone in both parameters: extra generators may go unused, and A ↦ A ⊕ 0 preserves mortality. The minimal undecidable cells therefore determine the rest. Before this series, the minimal mortality cells were M3(6), M5(4), M9(3), and M15(2) [Cassaigne et al. 2014]. With the constructions here and the reductions of Cassaigne, Halava, Harju, and Nicolas, they are

The smallest open cell#

M3(2) is the smallest unresolved two-generator cell. Pairs with a zero generator, with two invertible generators, or with a rank-one generator are decidable, which leaves one invertible and one rank-two generator, or two rank-two generators. For each of these a page here exhibits a family whose mortality is exactly an arithmetic orbit question: a deterministic p-adic orbit reaching 1 in the valuation guard, and shortcut-Collatz reachability of a given integer in shortcut-Collatz incidence. A decision procedure for M3(2) would decide these questions; an undecidability proof would have to compile a universal computation into orbits of this kind. Neither is known.

Problems#

The decision problems the constructions start from and end at.

  1. ≔Definition

    Matrix mortality

    Whether some nonempty product of finitely many integer matrices is zero, with the dimension d and the number k of matrices fixed.

    Used by

  2. ≔Definition

    Two-generator projective incidence

    Whether some product of two invertible rational 2 × 2 matrices moves a given column into the kernel of a given row.

    Used by
    Shortcut-Collatz incidence, Rank-two incidence compiler

  3. ≔Definition

    Shortcut-Collatz reachability

    Whether a given integer reaches 1 under x ↦ x/2 for even x and x ↦ (3x + 1)/2 for odd x.

    Used by
    Shortcut-Collatz incidence

Halting as a Word Equation#

The undecidable source: tag-system halting as equality of two concatenations with a fixed boundary.

  1. ∎Theorem

    Fixed-boundary correspondence

    Four of Neary’s word pairs with one fixed boundary word recognize halting of binary tag systems, for every word over the pairs.

    Used by
    Paired scalar series, Six-dimensional scalar compiler, M₃(5) is undecidable

A Word Equation as One Coefficient#

Matrix representations whose single coefficient vanishes exactly on solutions, with fewer generators or coordinates than the naive encoding.

  1. ≔Definition

    Word-pair matrices

    Three-dimensional integer matrices that multiply like pairs of words and detect equality of the two words in one entry.

    Used by
    Paired scalar series, Six-dimensional scalar compiler, M₃(5) is undecidable

  2. ≤Reduction

    Paired-role compression

    Four maps that agree in pairs on an r-dimensional subspace become two data maps and a toggle in dimension 2d − r, with the same coefficients.

    Used by
    Paired scalar series

  3. ⧉Construction

    Paired scalar series

    Three integer 4 × 4 matrices whose coefficient vanishes on a word exactly when the decoded tiles solve fixed-boundary correspondence; four dimensions are exact.

    Requires
    Fixed-boundary correspondence, Word-pair matrices, Paired-role compression
    Used by
    Tilted separator, M₄(4) is undecidable, M₉(2) is undecidable, Wrong-phase exclusion, Asymmetric separator, M₈(2) is undecidable

  4. ⧉Construction

    Six-dimensional scalar compiler

    Two-bit codewords spell the four tiles; sharing coordinates after the phase bit gives two 6 × 6 matrices with the same coefficients.

    Requires
    Fixed-boundary correspondence, Word-pair matrices
    Used by
    Z₆(2) is undecidable

One Coefficient as a Zero Product#

Separators and cuts that make a whole product vanish exactly when a coefficient does, including separator rows chosen to reduce dimension.

  1. ≤Reduction

    Rank-one separator

    A rank-one generator built from the boundary row and column turns a vanishing coefficient into a zero product; the converse covers every product.

    Requires
    Matrix mortality
    Used by
    M₃(5) is undecidable, M₄(4) is undecidable
    Instantiates
    Interface compression

  2. ≤Reduction

    Interface compression

    Mortality of transitions and factored cuts equals mortality of the transitions or of a path of small interface bridges.

    Requires
    Matrix mortality
    Used by
    Singular-return compression, Rank-two incidence compiler

  3. ≤Reduction

    Singular-return compression

    For a transition A with no vanishing power and a cut UO, the pair is mortal exactly when its returns O Aⁿ U are.

    Requires
    Interface compression
    Used by
    Finitely many return shapes, Valuation guard, M₉(2) is undecidable, M₈(2) is undecidable

  4. ⊢Lemma

    Finitely many return shapes

    If every return is a nonzero multiple of one of finitely many matrices, each occurring, the returns and those matrices are mortal together.

    Requires
    Singular-return compression
    Used by
    M₉(2) is undecidable, M₈(2) is undecidable

  5. ⧉Construction

    Tilted separator

    A separator row (1, q, q, q) whose zeros are exactly those of the paired series, by injectivity of the tilted code σ(x) + q·3^|x|.

    Requires
    Paired scalar series
    Used by
    Nine-dimensional return realization, Floor for uniform rows, M₉(2) is undecidable, Asymmetric separator

  6. ⊢Lemma

    Wrong-phase exclusion

    The rule-phase value of the asymmetric row never vanishes: a zero would force a forbidden period of the body’s code.

    Requires
    Paired scalar series
    Used by
    Asymmetric separator

  7. ⧉Construction

    Asymmetric separator

    The row (1, a, q, q) fixed by two linear conditions accepts only deletion-phase zeros; a leading toggle recovers every other witness.

    Requires
    Paired scalar series, Tilted separator, Wrong-phase exclusion
    Used by
    Eight-dimensional return realization, M₈(2) is undecidable

    Used by

    Instantiates

  3. ≤Reduction

    Both twelve-dimensional generators map into a ten-dimensional subspace; one appended letter turns every restricted zero into an original zero.

    Requires
    Binary prefix decoder
    Used by
    M₁₀(2) is undecidable

  4. ⧉Construction

    Nine-dimensional return realization

    A transition with Jordan blocks of sizes 3, 3, 2 and one eigenline whose returns are the three controls and multiples of the tilted separator.

    Requires
    Tilted separator
    Used by
    M₉(2) is undecidable

  5. ⊥Obstruction

    Floor for uniform rows

    At a benchmark source, every exact realization of the uniform-row return sequences needs nine dimensions, for every tail and slope.

    Requires
    Tilted separator

  6. ⧉Construction

    Eight-dimensional return realization

    Two nilpotent chains of length three, one static coordinate, and one eigenline realize a scaled toggle, both data controls, and the asymmetric separator.

    Used by

The Cell M₃(2)#

Exact equivalences between families of 3 × 3 pairs and arithmetic orbit questions; they do not settle the cell.

  1. ⧉Construction

    Valuation guard

    Two 3 × 3 matrices whose returns act by Möbius maps, admit one wait per step by a p-adic valuation, and are mortal exactly when a deterministic orbit reaches 1.

    Requires
    Singular-return compression
    Used by
    Immortality certificates

  2. ⊥Obstruction

    Immortality certificates

    Parity of the reset-defect numerator and valuation walls at primes dividing p − 1 prove large parameter regions of the guard immortal.

    Requires
    Valuation guard

  3. ≤Reduction

    Shortcut-Collatz incidence

    For each nonzero integer n, two fixed Möbius maps and a target depending on n have an incidence exactly when n reaches 1.

    Requires
    Two-generator projective incidence, Shortcut-Collatz reachability

  4. ≤Reduction

    Rank-two incidence compiler

    Every generic incidence instance becomes two rational 3 × 3 matrices of rank two, mortal exactly when the instance has a solution.

    Requires
    Two-generator projective incidence, Interface compression
    Instantiates
    Interface compression
