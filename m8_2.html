M₈(2): Mortality of Two 8 × 8 Integer Matrices

GPT-5.6 Sol · elicited by @eternalism_4eva

Matrix mortality for two 8 × 8 integer matrices is undecidable: M8(2).

Two constructions present the paired scalar series with two matrices, a transition A and a cut UO, whose returns OAnU are the toggle and the two data controls at times 0, 1, 2 and multiples of one rank-one separator afterwards. The dimension such a pair needs is a Hankel rank that depends on the separator’s row. A row that keeps exactly the zeros of the paired series gives dimension nine and proves M9(2); at a benchmark source, every row of that kind needs at least nine dimensions. Dimension eight forces a row that accepts zeros in only one of the two phases; prepending a toggle to a control word recovers every rejected zero, so the existence of a zero, and hence halting, is preserved.

Both reductions are primitive recursive and produce integer matrices. Zero padding gives Md(2) for every d ≥ 8. The previous bounds were M10(2) in this series and M15(2) in the literature. M7(2) remains open.

Known Stuff#

Two-generator mortality#

Md(2) asks whether some nonempty product of two given d × d integer matrices is zero; a single matrix counts as a pair with a repeated generator. Every word in the two matrices counts, with no grammar. Before this series the best bound was M15(2) [Cassaigne et al. 2014]. Generator packing applied to M3(5) gives M12(2), and the binary prefix decoder gives M10(2).

The paired source#

The paired scalar series has integer 4 × 4 controls Gb, Gc, T and boundary column c. For a Neary tag system with width β ≥ 3 and body B, put

ρ=3β,μ=σ(M)=5ρ−12,t=3|M|=3ρ, W=H(B),K=3|W|+3,V=σ(1W10).

Here M = 10β is the boundary word, H the letter code, and 1W10 the lower word of the tile Rc, so K and V are its length power and ternary value. The constructions below use the column after one toggle,

u=Tc=(μ,0,t,−1)T.

For a control word z, Gzu = GzTc is one of the two phase columns: with X the decoded upper word followed by M, Y the decoded lower word, and h = σ(X) − σ(Y),

Gzu= { (h,−3|Y|,3|X|,0)Trule phase, (h,0,3|X|,−3|Y|)Tdeletion phase.

The lower word Y is a concatenation of the tiles’ lower words 0, 110, and 1W10; no legality of the decoded computation is assumed. Some nonempty control word has h = 0 exactly when the tag system halts. Prepending T to z switches the phase and keeps X and Y.

Returns of one transition and one cut#

Take a transition A, maps U from ℚ4 into its space and O back, and the cut C = UO. The returns are Γn = OAnU. By singular-return compression, if no power of A vanishes, then {A, C} is mortal exactly when {Γn : n ≥ 0} is. If moreover every return is a nonzero multiple of one of finitely many matrices and each of them occurs, the returns may be replaced by those matrices (finitely many return shapes).

Both constructions aim at the four shapes T, Gb, Gc, and a rank-one separator ur for a row r with first entry 1. Every control fixes e1, so rGz is never zero, and Gzu = 0 already implies rGzu = 0. The argument of M4(4) then shows that, when ru ≠ 0,

Mortal{T,Gb,Gc,ur} ⇔ ∃z≠ε:rGzu=0.

The whole design problem is therefore to choose r so that the right side is equivalent to halting, and to realize the four shapes as returns in as few dimensions as possible.

Realizing a return sequence#

Finding (A, U, O) with prescribed matrices OAnU is the classical realization problem of linear systems theory: the least possible dimension is the rank of the block Hankel matrix [Γi+j]i,j≥0 [Ho–Kalman 1966]. The lower bound is immediate, since Γi+j = (OAi)(AjU) factors through the state space.

The sequences here are geometric after time two: Γn = ξ2−nS for n ≥ 3, with ξ ≠ 0 and S of rank one. Extrapolate the tail backward and subtract it:

Nn=Γn−ξ2−nS (n=0,1,2), Nn=0(n≥3).

Splitting A into its nilpotent and invertible parts splits any realization into one of the extrapolated tail, which needs at least rank S = 1 dimension, and one of the residuals N0, N1, N2. The nilpotent part is a sum of Jordan chains, and a chain of length ℓ contributes only at times 0, …, ℓ−1. The minimal numbers of chains are

  • c3 = rank N2 chains of length three;
  • c2 chains of length two, the rank of N1 as a map from ker N2 to ℚ4/im N2, which is the part of N1 that the longer chains cannot supply;
  • c1 chains of length one, for what remains of N0.

The least dimension is 3c3 + 2c2 + c1 + 1, the Hankel rank of the sequence.

New Stuff#

Result#

Aim the returns at Gc at time two, Gb at time one, a multiple of T at time zero, and multiples of ur afterwards. The data control Gc has rank three, with kernel spanned by

κ=(K−3V,3,0,−K)T.

Subtracting a rank-one tail term ur can lower its rank to two only if rκ = 0. Each chain count therefore turns into a linear condition on the row r:

Separator rowc3c2c1DimensionZeros kept
e1T, the paired row3at least 10all
(1, q, q, q) with rκ = 02109all
(1, a, q, q) with rκ = rη = 02018deletion phase only

The paired row fails because e1Tκ = K − 3V ≠ 0. A row keeps every zero of the paired series only if it vanishes on both phase columns of every solution, where h = 0 and |X| = |Y|; that forces its last three entries to be equal. Such a uniform row can satisfy rκ = 0, which fixes its slope and gives dimension nine. Removing the chain of length two needs the second condition rη = 0, where Gcη = Gbκ, and no nonzero uniform row satisfies both conditions. A row satisfying both exists once the rule-phase entry is allowed to differ. It rejects the rule-phase members of solutions, and prepending a toggle turns every such solution into an accepted deletion-phase one.

The table explains the two designs; the proofs below construct the realizations directly and do not depend on the count.

Theorem (M8(2)). A primitive-recursive map sends every program to two 8 × 8 integer matrices that are mortal exactly when the program halts. Hence Md(2) is undecidable for every d ≥ 8.

Theorem (M9(2)). A primitive-recursive map sends every program to two 9 × 9 integer matrices that are mortal exactly when the program halts. The separator of this construction vanishes on exactly the control words at which the paired series, after one appended toggle, vanishes.

Dimension nine: keeping every zero#

A uniform row (1, s, s, s) reads both phase columns the same way:

(1,s,s,s)Gzu = Fs(X)−Fs(Y), Fs(x)=σ(x)+s3|x|.

Tilted codes#

The tilted code Fs is injective on binary words whenever s < −3/2. A word of length n has (3n − 1)/2 ≤ σ(x) ≤ 3n − 1, so Fs maps the words of each length into an interval, and for s < −3/2 these intervals are disjoint and move down as n grows. Within one length, Fs differs from the injective σ by a constant. Hence (1, s, s, s)Gzu = 0 exactly when X = Y, which is exactly when the paired coefficient of zT vanishes. The row keeps every zero, only shifted by one appended toggle.

The slope required by rκ = 0 is

q9=K−3VK−3,

and the syntax of the tag source gives q9 < −3/2 for every body. The tilted separator is u(1, q9, q9, q9).

The nine-dimensional realization#

The target sequence is

Γn= { T,n=0, Gb,n=1, Gc,n=2, νn−3S9,n≥3,

with ν ≠ 0 and S9 a nonzero multiple of the tilted separator. Because rκ = 0, a suitably normalized tail makes rank N2 = 2, which gives two chains of length three. The row is uniform, so rη ≠ 0 and one chain of length two remains; the tail is tuned so that no chain of length one is needed. With the eigenline of the tail this gives

A∼J3(0)⊕J3(0)⊕J2(0)⊕[ν],3+3+2+1=9.

An exact symbolic program constructs this factorization, and Lean checks the resulting rational 9 × 9 transition, 9 × 4 input, and 4 × 9 output and every return. The denominators and pivots are nonzero whenever β > 0 and B contains b, which holds for every body produced by the universal source.

The floor for uniform rows#

Fix the benchmark source β = 3, B = bb. Consider every return sequence equal to T, Gb, Gc at times 0, 1, 2 and to any rational multiple of νn−3u(1, q, q, q) afterwards, for any rational ν and any rational q < −3/2. Every exact realization of such a sequence has dimension at least nine. Zero-preserving uniform rows therefore cannot give dimension eight with this design, even after retuning the tail. The bound concerns this family of exact realizations; it says nothing about M8(2) itself.

Dimension eight: keeping some zero#

A row r = (1, a, q, q) reads the two phase columns differently:

rGzu= { Fq(X)−Fa(Y)in rule phase, Fq(X)−Fq(Y)in deletion phase.

With

η=(K−3V+48,0,0,27−K)T,

the two conditions rκ = rη = 0 determine both slopes:

q=K−3V+48K−27=−1−θ, a=16+9q=7−9θ, θ=σ(W)3|W|−1.

Here θ is the periodic ternary value of W. Since W begins with 1 and contains 0, 1/2 < θ < 1, so −2 < q < −3/2 and a > −2. By the tilted-code injectivity, the deletion-phase value vanishes exactly when X = Y. The rule-phase value compares two different codes, and its possible zeros are the new obligation.

The wrong-phase exclusion#

The rule-phase value Fq(X) − Fa(Y) never vanishes, for any upper word X and any lower word Y built from the lower tiles. The proof uses one more property of the universal source: every body it emits begins with bcb, so

W=H(b)H(c)H(b)⋯=10β1110β1⋯.

Suppose Fq(X) = Fa(Y).

One word is a suffix of the other

The slopes have denominator 3|W| − 1, which is prime to 3. Multiply by it and reduce modulo 3m, where m is the shorter length: the terms with 3|X| and 3|Y| disappear, so σ(X) ≡ σ(Y) modulo 3m. The last m ternary digits agree, so the shorter word is a suffix of the longer.

The lower word is longer

If |X| = |Y|, then X = Y and q = a = 16 + 9q, so q = −2, which is excluded. If X = pY with p nonempty, cancelling Y gives Fq(p) = a; but Fq(p) ≤ 3|p|(1 + q) − 1 < −2 < a. So Y = pX with p nonempty.

A period equation

With k = |p|, cancelling X gives q = σ(p) + a3k, and substituting q = −1 − θ, a = 7 − 9θ,

θ=σ(10p)+13k+2−1.

Clearing denominators and reducing modulo 3, the fact that W ends in 1 forces p to end in 0. Let p+ be p with that final 0 changed to 1 and put P = 10p+, so that σ(P) = σ(10p) + 1. Then θ is the periodic value of both W and P, and cross-multiplying shows σ(WP) = σ(PW). By injectivity of σ,

WP=PW.

Commuting words are powers of a common word, so P is a prefix of the infinite repetition Wω and Pω = Wω. In particular p+ consists of the letters of Wω in positions 2, …, k+1, counting from 0.

No length survives

After its first two letters, W reads 0β−11110β1⋯. A lower word is a concatenation of 0, 110, and 1W10, so its first 1 is followed by 10. The prefix p of Y agrees with p+ except in its last letter.

  1. k ≤ β − 1: then p+ = 0k, which does not end in 1.
  2. k = β: then P = H(b), whose repetition continues 10, while W continues 11.
  3. k = β + 1: then p = 0β−110, whose first 1 is followed by 0, so p is not a prefix of a lower word.
  4. k = β + 2: then P = H(bcc). Its repetition has 1 in position β+4, where W has the first 0 of its second H(b).
  5. k ≥ β + 3: then p begins 0β−1111, again not a prefix of a lower word.

Cases 3 and 5 use the lower-word grammar and cases 2 and 4 the period; neither argument alone suffices.

Recovering every witness#

The row r therefore vanishes exactly on the deletion-phase columns with X = Y. Each such zero is a zero of the paired series. Conversely, if z is a nonempty control word with X = Y in rule phase, then Tz has the same X and Y in deletion phase. The empty word gives ru ≠ 0, because its upper word is M and its lower word is empty. Hence

∃z≠ε:rGzu=0⇔the tag system halts.

This is existential zero transport: the set of zero words changes, but whether it is empty does not.

The eight-dimensional realization#

Define

λ=2q+1,h=6λ(3ρ−1),ξ=3ρ+1+ρh,S=hur, Δ=K((λ−2)ρ−λ)+26λ+72, γ=K(λ(27ρ3−1)+18ρ2+6ρ)Δ.

Here λ < −2, ρ ≥ 27, and K > 27, so |ρh| < 2 and ξ > 0. Moreover

Δ<K(2−4ρ)+20<0, λ(27ρ3−1)+18ρ2+6ρ<−54ρ3+18ρ2+6ρ+2<0,

so γ > 0 and h ≠ 0; no scaling or denominator vanishes. The target sequence is

Γn= { γT,n=0, Gb,n=1, Gc,n=2, ξ2−nS,n≥3,

with residuals

N0=γT−ξ2S,N1=Gb−ξS,N2=Gc−S.

The normalization of h makes rank N2 = 2, with kernel spanned by κ and

z=(3ρ−16,0,ρ,−13)T,Gcz=u;

the choice of ξ makes N1 map that kernel into the image of N2. The residuals therefore need two chains of length three and one of length one, and the tail needs one eigenline: 3 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 8.

The factorization#

The chains can be written down without choosing a basis by a rank test. Let E be the 4 × 2 matrix with columns e1 and e3, put Li = NiE, and let

Λ=[ 00−q/3(3ρ−λ)/6 001/3ρ ], Q2=[ 1a+K(3ρ−λ)/60(3ρ−1)/2 0ρK13ρ ].

Then ΛL2 = I2, and N2 = L2Q2 is a rank factorization through the first and third coordinates. The other factors correct the residuals one time step at a time:

Q1=Λ(N1−L1Q2),N0′=N0−L0Q2−L1Q1, f=(0,0,0,γ),j=−3N0′z/γ,Q0=Λ(N0′−jf).

The row f is the second row of N0, and jf is the part of the time-zero remainder outside the image of L2: the chain of length one. Substitution gives

N2=L2Q2, N1=L1Q2+L2Q1, N0=L0Q2+L1Q1+L2Q0+jf.

Write the state as (x0, x1, x2, y, g) with xi ∈ ℚ2 and y, g ∈ ℚ. The blocks x0, x1, x2 are the three levels of the two chains of length three, y is the chain of length one, and g is the eigenline:

A(x0,x1,x2,y,g)=(0,x0,x1,0,ξ−1g), Uv=(Q2v,Q1v,Q0v,fv,ξ2hrv), O(x0,x1,x2,y,g)=L0x0+L1x1+L2x2+jy+ug.

Each application of A moves the chain blocks one level down, kills y, and scales g by ξ−1. The factor identities give Γ0, Γ1, Γ2. From time three on only g survives and gives ξ2−nS, and the eigenvalue ξ−1 ≠ 0 shows that no power of A vanishes.

Two matrices#

In both realizations the transition has a nonzero eigenvalue, so no power vanishes and singular-return compression identifies mortality of {A, UO} with mortality of the returns. Every return is a nonzero multiple of T, Gb, Gc, or the separator, and times 0, 1, 2, 3 supply all four, so the returns may be replaced by these four shapes. By the separator argument, the four shapes are mortal exactly when some nonempty control word has zero separator coefficient. In dimension nine that happens exactly when the paired series has a zero; in dimension eight, by existential transport, exactly when the tag system halts.

In dimension nine the separator coefficient of z vanishes exactly when the paired coefficient of zT does. In dimension eight the two sets of zero words differ, and only their nonemptiness agrees.

Integer matrices and effectivity#

Denominator clearing#

Both realizations have rational entries. Multiply each generator by a nonzero integer di that clears its denominators; the two factors may differ. For every nonempty word,

G^i1⋯G^in=(∏k=1ndik)Gi1⋯Gin,G^i=diGi∈ℤd×d,

so the integer product is zero exactly when the rational product is. For a computable reduction the entries must also be computed without searching. They are evaluated as unreduced numerator–denominator pairs, for which addition, multiplication, division by a nonzero value, and fixed powers are primitive recursive without any greatest-common-divisor computation; one common denominator per generator is accumulated from them. Neither construction uses a numerical rank test, a chosen pivot, or a basis search at run time.

The universal reduction#

Let CodeHalts(e) state that mathlib’s universal partial recursive code e halts on input zero; this predicate is not computable. A fixed universal two-tag system is compiled through Cook’s cyclic tag construction and Neary’s compiler into a width β > 2 and a body Be, primitive recursive in e, that is long enough, has length divisible by β − 1, and begins with bcb. The checked tag theorem gives

CodeHalts(e)⇔the tag system (β,Be) halts⇔the emitted integer pair is mortal.

The composed map is primitive recursive, so it is a many-one reduction from a noncomputable predicate, and both mortality problems are undecidable. Adjoining a zero block, Gi ↦ Gi ⊕ 0n, is primitive recursive and preserves and reflects every zero product, which gives every larger dimension.

M7(2) remains open. The floor for uniform rows concerns exact realizations of one family of return sequences; the eight-dimensional construction avoids it by changing both the return sequence and the set of zero words.

Formal scope#

For both theorems Lean checks the source compiler, the separator coefficients for every control word, every return, unrestricted physical products including transitions before the first and after the last cut, denominator clearing, primitive recursiveness, the many-one reduction, noncomputability, and padding. No proof depends on a finite search, floating-point arithmetic, or an unproved invertibility.

The chain-count table and the necessity of rκ = rη = 0 in dimension eight are recorded in the audit and are not formalized; the proofs do not use them. The statement that Λ is a left inverse of L2 is checked by the symbolic program; Lean checks the factor identities directly. The floor for uniform rows is formalized at the benchmark source only. The symbolic programs’ bounded word enumerations are auxiliary.

Priority#

Searches of public literature through 24 July 2026 found no proof of Md(2) for any d < 15 outside this series [Cassaigne et al. 2014]. M9(2) was completed on 31 August 2026 and M8(2) on 5 September 2026. Return realizations, rank-one separators, alphabet reduction, and denominator clearing are prior techniques. Priority is claimed for the two theorems and their constructions, not for those techniques. Machine checking is not external peer review.
